# Sholynk Voice Reference: The Author's Tone

**What this document is.** A codified description of the author's natural voice,
distilled from a first-person writing sample (a set of candid answers about lesson
planning and educational tooling). Every article on Sholynk Tech should read as if
written in this voice, applied under the **hybrid rule** below so that the site's
evidence-first standard is preserved.

Companion documents: `docs/AUTHOR_PUBLISHING_GUIDE.md` (layout slots and house
style), `article_stories/README.md` (mechanics).

---

## 1. The voice, in one sentence

Formal but personal: the author speaks from their own position ("I", "for me"),
opens answers directly, builds long clause-chained sentences joined by classical
connectives, enumerates exhaustively, and names concrete failure modes instead of
vague criticism.

## 2. Voice markers (with evidence from the source sample)

1. **First-person authority.** The author writes from personal practice and
   preference, never from a detached institutional stance.
   - "In preparing a lesson plan, I use the scheme of work intended for the term."
   - "Well, for me I'd prefer a smart app…"
   - "An app is deemed efficient for me if it handles the above-listed challenges…"

2. **Direct, conversational-formal openers.** Answers begin without throat-clearing.
   - "Absolutely,…" / "Well, for me…" / "To begin with,…"

3. **Signature connectives.** Prefer these transitions where logic allows:
   - *To begin with,* (opening an argument)
   - *However,* (introducing the counter-case)
   - *hence* (drawing the consequence)
   - *by the time* (temporal consequence)
   - *in cases where*, *given* (conditional framing)

4. **Long, additive sentence construction.** Ideas chain through commas and
   semicolons, often with parallel repetition, rather than being broken into short
   declarative beats.
   - "…has the capacity to identify…, has the capacity to track…, has the capacity
     to suggest…"
   - "…there'll always be a need to correct some text and update certain statements
     that make up the content of the plan."

5. **Formal, Latinate diction.** "Susceptible to misplacement", "deemed efficient",
   "preliminary point of discussion", "given the grammatical construct". No slang,
   almost no contractions.

6. **Enumerative habit.** Complete inventories written out as comma-joined noun
   phrases: "the topic title, subtopics, references, introduction, body, conclusion,
   evaluation, and assignments."

7. **Friction-honest pragmatism.** Problems are named as concrete failure modes,
   never abstractions: "lags, crashes, hallucinations, storage and battery
   mismanagement, and unnecessary hikes in subscription fees."

8. **British register.** British English spelling and idiom throughout (consistent
   with the house rule in `AUTHOR_PUBLISHING_GUIDE.md`).

## 3. The hybrid application rule

The voice is applied **by zone**, not uniformly:

| Zone | Register |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | Author's voice: first-person stance, direct opening, signature connectives. |
| Transitions between sections | Author's voice: *To begin with, However, hence, in cases where.* |
| Evaluative passages (opinion, judgement, advice) | Author's voice: "For me…", "The approach I would recommend…", longer chained sentences. |
| Conclusion | Author's voice: a personal synthesis, not a summary. |
| Definitions, mechanisms, facts, figures | Neutral register: precise third-person exposition. No "I" attached to a verifiable claim. |
| Attributed claims and statistics | Neutral register: the source speaks; the author does not insert themselves. |
| `directAnswer`, `keyTakeaways`, `sources` | Neutral register: these slots are quoted by search and AI systems and must stand alone as clean statements. |
| `hook`, `faqs` | Light voice allowed: a direct opener or "I would suggest…" where it reads naturally. |

Standing constraints from the source sample that are **already** house rules and
must not be relaxed: strictly British English; no em dashes between words; no
banned marketing register; facts, dates, sources and links are never altered by a
voice pass.

## 4. Sentence-construction guidance

- Default to the author's longer additive sentences in voice zones: join related
  clauses with commas and semicolons, and use parallel repetition for lists of
  capabilities or effects.
- Keep the short declarative beat only where a claim needs to land as a verdict
  ("Frameworks are rented. Fundamentals are owned.").
- Avoid turning every sentence into a long one; alternate. A paragraph of three
  chained sentences followed by one short verdict reads like the author.
- Never introduce first-person claims of specific lived experience the author has
  not made. Stance markers ("For me,", "In my view,", "I would recommend") are
  safe; invented anecdotes are not.

## 5. Pre-publication voice checklist

- [ ] Does the introduction open directly, with the author present?
- [ ] Do transitions use the signature connectives where natural?
- [ ] Are evaluative passages written in first person ("For me…", "I would…")?
- [ ] Do facts, figures and attributed claims remain in neutral register?
- [ ] Are long additive sentences alternating with short verdicts?
- [ ] Are concrete failure modes named instead of abstractions?
- [ ] Is the piece still strictly British English, with no em dashes?
- [ ] Have all facts, sources, links, slugs and slots survived the voice pass unchanged?
